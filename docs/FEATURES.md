# everything ncut does

the long list. the short one is in the [readme](../README.md).

- multi-track timeline: video, image, text, shape and audio clips. move, trim (drag an edge with several clips selected and they all trim together; `[` and `]` or the toolbar trim the start or the end to the playhead), trim mode (y or the toolbar): drag an edge to ripple trim, where two clips touch to move the edit, the middle of a clip to slip, alt to slide, shift on an edge to stretch; ctrl + drag an edge ripples without trim mode; q / w ripple trim to the playhead; u picks the edit at the playhead and , . trim the selected edge by frames; the preview shows the frames on both sides while you trim, alt+left / alt+right nudge the selected clips one frame, shift ten, a held key is one undo step; with a selected edge they trim it like , and . (one undo step per repeat); up / down go to the previous / next clip start or end on the tracks of the selected clips (every track when none is selected), drop a clip, text, shape, effect or camera from the media panel inbetween two clips (the clips after it move right to make room, in one undo step), drop a clip on the border between two tracks and a new track opens there, split, ripple delete, duplicate, copy/paste, snapping, markers, markers on the beats of a song, groups, separate sound: picture and sound as two grouped clips, j and l cuts, a badge when they drift out of sync, join sound back (ctrl+l or the clip menu. a selected pair trims, moves and stretches together, alt+click one side to trim it alone. "separate sound on drop" in the settings, off by default, makes a video with sound you put on the timeline arrive as the two clips), freeze frame (the still keeps the mask, the effects and the fades of the clip, and a picture whose sound is separate cuts its sound clip the same way), undo/redo
- in and out: i and o mark the timeline range (o includes the frame on screen), x marks the clip under the playhead, shift+x clears. the range is shaded on the ruler, its brackets drag and snap. shift+i / shift+o go there, shift+space plays in to out, loop loops inside it. ; lifts what is between in and out (the gap stays), ' extracts it (every unlocked track closes up). the export can render just in to out. the keyframe key is shift+k now.
- source view: a click on a media card shows it in the source view (shift+v there and back), with its own playhead, j k l, frame steps and a bar with its sound. i and o mark the part to use; a drag, a double click or + takes only that part, picture only / sound only one half. f on a clip opens its media at the same frame, f in the source view goes back. the mouse over a video card skims it. side by side shows both on a wide preview.
- three point edits: insert (f9), overwrite (f10), replace, fit to the range and append put the marked part of the source view at the in point or the playhead. the crosshair in a track header picks where picture and sound go. insert moves the clips after it on every unlocked track.
- tracks: drag the bottom edge of a track header to change the track height (shift: every track of its kind, double click: normal height), or use alt+= / alt+- and the taller tracks / shorter tracks buttons in the toolbar. 28 to 240 px, a view setting, not an undo step. drag a header to move the track up or down inside its kind (a video track higher up draws on top), or use the track menu. sync lock is a switch per track, on by default: ripple, insert and extract on other tracks move it too. off, it stays where it is for edits on other tracks, and the clips that end up out of sync are named. short tracks fold the header to one row
- the properties of a clip are split in tabs: basic (name, timing, the main settings, transform, attach, speed, fades), look (style, color, grade, mask, matte, effects, 3d, bend), animate (keyframes, motion, path, repeat, transition) and sound (volume, pan, crossfade, tempo). "all" is the one long list. the tab you pick stays while you switch clips, left / right arrows move between tabs
- every number in the properties is a slider: drag it sideways (shift is finer, ctrl lands on round steps), click to type (1920/2 works, a comma works), double click to put it back to its rest value. the fill shows where the value sits in its usual range, a thick end means it is past it, dragging on goes further. a drag is one undo step and esc takes it back. number fields show the real value instead of cutting it short (a 70 frame clip at 60 fps shows 1.1667 s), and leaving a field changes nothing if the text is the same as what was shown. with several clips selected, position, scale, rotation, opacity, depth, tilt, turn, volume, fades and effect strength work for all of them: a drag moves each one by the same amount (scale by the same factor), a typed number sets them all
- a plain color card is always the first thing in the media tab, in every project, with nothing to import: a full frame picture of one color (pick the color on the card, change it later in the properties). a background, a flash, or a plane for 3d
- real-time preview on a canvas, with on-canvas move / scale / rotate, zoom and pan, guides, and a quality switch (full / half / quarter) for heavy timelines. preview speed from 0.25x to 2x and j / k / l shuttle in both directions. click the time in the transport, or press g, to type a time: 1:30:00, 90f or 12.5s goes there, + or - moves by it (from the timeline it moves the selected clips or the selected edge). right click the time for timecode, frames or seconds, the ruler follows
- scopes: shift+w or the scopes button shows waveform, parade, vectorscope (skin line, 75% targets) and histogram next to the preview, on 0..100. live while playing, exact while paused (every pixel, like the export). alt over the picture reads one pixel. the scene view measures the output. closed, nothing runs.
- keyframes on every number of a clip, each with its own diamond, previous / next key and easing: position, scale, rotation, opacity, volume, pan, effect settings, camera, and also corner radius, crop edges, color correction, the grade wheels, color key, mask (position, size, turn, soft edge, corners), 3d thickness, how lit (3d), a text's size, spacing, line gap, outline, shadow, depth, gradient angle, curve, wrap and box, and a shape's size, corners, inner radius, gradient angle and outline width. every number has a key list of its own, a key on one number never touches another. a number inside something that has to be switched on first (mask, color key, outline, shadow, depth, gradient, lit) can be keyed once that is on. tools for a whole animation: reverse it, fit it to the clip, repeat it, ping pong, copy it to another clip, or add a wiggle (a seeded shake on any value)
- keyframe panel: shift+u, its toolbar button or the animate tab shows one row per animated value of the selected clips under the tracks. the diamond shape shows the ease. select keyframes across values and clips: a drag moves only the selected ones, alt+drag stretches them around the playhead. with the focus in the panel, delete, ctrl+c / x / v / a, alt+left / alt+right, enter and esc act on keyframes only, never on clips. paste puts the earliest copied keyframe at the playhead. closed, nothing of it runs
- graph: shift+g or "graph" in the panel head shows one line per animated value, as a value graph or a speed graph. drag keyframes in time and value, and drag their handles for a custom ease (influence in %, speed in /s). easy ease (shift+e, and f9 while the panel has the keyframes), ease into (shift+f9), ease out of (ctrl+shift+f9) and join handles are in the bar and the menu. alt+up / alt+down raise or lower the selected keyframes a step. f frames the selected keyframes, and the bar has fit all, fit selected and auto fit. the named eases play as before
- a stretch per axis on top of scale: scale x and scale y make the box wider or taller (the picture or the letters stretch with it, a shape's outline keeps an even width), scale z is the depth of a 3d solid or of extruded text. all three can be keyed. a clip attached to a stretched clip follows its scale only, never its stretch
- bpm: a clip with sound has a tempo. ncut listens and finds it (detect), or type it, or tap along, with half / double time and a "first beat" nudge, and the beats show as ticks on the clip. the keyframes of any clip can then follow that beat: loop the first 1/2, 1, 2, 4, 8 or 16 beats until the clip ends, ping pong, fit the whole animation to a number of beats, or snap every keyframe to the nearest beat, half beat or quarter beat. edit the first round of a loop and press loop again to carry it through. effect clips follow the tempo too (pulse on the beat in their inspector): pick whose tempo (or the sound under the effect), a cycle of 1/4 to 16 beats, and a pulse that hits at full strength on every cycle line and falls off until the next one. effects that repeat on their own (flash, zoom pulse, glitch, static, scanlines, hue wash and more) also have a lock that makes one of their cycles one beat cycle. it is worked out when a frame is drawn, so it stays right when the effect or the music is moved, trimmed or split
- 3d, set up like a small game engine scene: any clip can become a plane, box, ball, tube, cone, pyramid, ring, capsule, prism, hexagon, star or heart in one shared 3d space, mixed freely with flat 2d layers. camera clips move through it (with ready-made moves: push in, orbit, dolly zoom, handheld), a scene view lets you orbit around the set with move / rotate / scale tools, driven like blender by default (middle drag orbits, g / r / s move, rotate and scale without holding a button, x / y / z pick the axis, a typed number sets the amount, shift + d copies, a left drag draws a selection box, the numpad picks the view) or like unity with a switch in the settings (left drag orbits, w e r pick the tool, alt + drag leaves a copy); ctrl snaps to a grid in both while a small inset shows what the camera sees, distance fog fades far things out, lit clips take their light from the scene (the background, the filled 2d shapes under the lowest 3d clip and the fog; pictures, video and text are not read) or from a light of your own (sky, horizon, ground and a sun). light only darkens or tints: a white surrounding leaves a clip as it is, on the default black background a lit clip gets darker, and its how lit slider softens that, and the camera has depth of field: pick what is sharp, keyframe it to pull the focus, or drag the focus plane right in the scene view
- skyboxes: import a cube map (one cross or strip picture, or six pictures named like px, posx, right or rt) under project > sky, or press "use as sky" when an import looks like one. it is drawn behind every layer, far away, turns with the camera and gives lit 3d clips its light (a dome and a sun measured from it). turn, brightness and blur can be set, the six sides can be swapped and turned by hand.
- bend a flat picture clip (a 2d clip or a plane) like a cinema screen or a rolled page: an angle (keyable) along its width or height, and where it stays flat. bend several clips as one: a row of pictures, a wall of cards or a text split into letters roll round together into one surface, up to a closed ring, and the bend stays editable and keyable for all of them at once. 2d clips bend too, seen straight on
- attach clips to each other (or to the camera, or to an invisible "empty") so they move, rotate and scale together, see the whole set as a tree in the layers tab, and make any clip look at the camera or another clip. give any clip a pivot, the point it rotates and scales around (a shoulder, a hinge, the middle of a clock): pick one of nine spots, drag it on the preview, or put it where the clip meets the clip it is attached to. with attach this builds a rig: an arm that rotates at the shoulder, the elbow and the wrist. setting a pivot keeps the clip in place at the playhead (a clip that looks at something looks from its pivot, so a big picture can turn a little), and keys, stretch, look at and the ready-made motions all rotate around it. arrange a selection in 3d in one click: ring, row, stack, fan, grid, spiral, sphere, scatter, wave, stairs. put a clip in front of the camera, frame it with the camera, lay it flat or drop it on the floor. repeat a clip: a row of copies, each a step further in position, depth, turn, size, opacity or time (an echo, a staircase, a tunnel). send a clip along a path in one click: circle, figure eight, spiral, swing, hop, zigzag, drift, written as ordinary keyframes, and it can rotate to point the way it moves
- motion blur many ways: a real shutter as an effect clip (centered, trailing, leading), per-clip motion blur that follows keyframes and motions in three kinds (smooth, trail, ghost) on 2d and 3d clips, echo / frame blend, and directional / zoom / spin / tilt shift / lens / soft focus / rgb trail blur
- 95 effects (color, blur, distortion, stylize, motion, looks like sketch, neon edges, hologram, oil paint, glass and led screen, and overlays like snow, rain, confetti, bokeh, light rays, lens flare) as effect clips over everything below (an adjustment layer), or put on one clip only (a blur on a text really goes soft at the edges, a shake or a glitch moves its outline too), a chroma key effect that makes one color see-through (on a track of its own it keys the video or picture right under it), with favorites, and 87 transitions (fades, slides, pushes, wipes, 3d turns, glitches) you drop on a cut, or put on every cut of a track at once (one kind or shuffled)
- per-clip color (brightness, contrast, saturation, warmth, hue), green screen with spill cleanup, masks with a soft edge (box, oval, half, strip, star, heart), blend modes, crop, rounded corners. split screen in one click: side by side, stacked, grid, one big, picture in picture
- matte: one clip shows through another. in the look tab, after mask, pick a clip and how it cuts: alpha, luma or inverted. the matte clip is not drawn on its own, but keeps its keys, effects and 3d and plays at its own time. a blur on it softens the edge. "use clip above" takes the picture clip on the nearest track above, at the same time, in one step (a search action does it too). a mark on both clips shows the pair. attach them to move them together
- grade: a grade section in the look tab of every video, image, text and shape clip, after color. four wheels (lift, gamma, gain, offset), each with a level, and seven curves (luma, red, green, blue, hue vs sat, hue vs hue, luma vs sat). the wheel numbers can be keyed, the curves stay the same for the whole clip. "grade on" turns it off to compare, the export follows the switch. copy grade / paste grade match shots. several clips share grade on, the four levels, paste and reset. a grade effect clip grades everything below it, or one target clip
- luts: import a .cube file (1d, 3d or both, up to 65 points) and it shows as a lut card in the media tab. the grade has a lut row: mix, lut first / lut last, made for 0 to 255 / 16 to 235, and take the lut off. drag a card onto a clip, or double click it, press enter or +, to put it on the selected clips. on a grade effect it covers everything below. save as .cube writes the color and grade of a clip at the playhead as a 33 point lut, with no key, masks or effects. a missing lut draws the clip without it. removing the media takes it off every grade that uses it
- text: 29 bundled fonts plus system fonts and your own font files, outline, second outline, hollow letters, shadow, gradient, block depth (real thickness when the text stands in 3d), underline and strike, a box per line, curved and wrapped text, per-letter animations in, out and looping, karaoke highlighting, split a text into letters, words or lines (every piece becomes its own clip, right where it stood, ready to be arranged in 3d), and running numbers that count up or down, as plain numbers or as a timer (m:ss) with steady digits
- captions: import .srt / .vtt, split a long text into captions, export .srt
- an outline on a 3d solid goes around the solid and along its edges
- shapes with outlines that draw themselves on, and ready-made clip motions in, out and looping (some just for 3d: turntable, tumble, fly in)
- fades, per-clip volume, speed and opacity. swap the media of a clip and keep everything else (a sound clip takes any media with sound, a picture whose sound is separate swaps its sound clip too)
- crossfades: shift+t (or ctrl+shift+d) crossfades the sound at the selected edit, 0.25 s centered, equal power, and again removes it. it plays the media past the edit, and the picture is left as it is. a box under the transition band shows it: drag an end to change the length (alt: one end only) or the middle to move it across the edit. a click picks it: delete removes it, , and . change it a frame each side, right click opens its menu. the sound tab has a crossfade section (length, place, shape), and the fades have a sound shape (equal power, linear, smooth). the track menu crossfades every edit of a track or removes them all. a transition has "crossfade the sound too"
- a mixer tab (shift+6): a fader in db, pan, mute and solo per track, an output strip, meters with an over light; clip pan, keyable. solo is for listening only: never saved, never in the export, and the transport and the export dialog say when it is on
- loudness: the mixer has a loudness strip, live while playing and close, not exact: momentary, short term, integrated, loudness range and true peak. measure mix renders the sound and gives the exact numbers. the export has a loudness target (off, -14 streaming, -16 podcast, -23 ebu r128, -24 atsc a/85 or custom) and a peak limit (true peak, -1 dbtp): it gains the mix to the target, limits only what would pass the ceiling and tells what came out. off is the old file. saved per browser, and no preset sets it
- cascade a selection: start the clips one after the other (left to right, middle outwards, random ...) and let them fly in or out (scatter, rise, fall, zoom, spin, from the depth). made for a text split into letters
- cut to the music: mark the beats of a song, then lay the selected clips in a row, fit them to the markers (every beat, every 2nd, every 4th) or split a clip at every marker
- export to mp4 or webm with webcodecs at up to 240 fps, gif, or audio only (wav / ogg), whole timeline or a range, with one click presets. no upload, no watermark. what you see in the preview is what gets exported
- advanced export: ctrl+shift+e opens the export with the advanced part open. video codec (auto, h.264, h.265, av1, vp9), bitrate, key frame interval, size (fit to the size or fill the size), see-through background (webm and png), and the audio codec, audio bitrate and sample rate. a choice this machine cannot do is greyed with its reason, the summary shows the size estimate, and back to auto puts every choice back. five new presets: youtube 1080p, youtube 4k, vimeo, vertical reels and master. every default is the file the export always made
- export a picture: png or jpg of the first frame (or the frame at a marker or at any time), in the project's full size or smaller
- fast export: longer videos are rendered in parts by several workers at once and joined into one file, 1440p and 4k go to the graphics card's encoder at any frame rate, gifs are built on up to 8 workers, big files are written to disk while they are made instead of sitting in memory
- smooth scrubbing: dragging the playhead forward through a video shows about 50 to 60 pictures a second, also with 4k files (a decoder of its own keeps going while you drag, the resting picture is exact). backwards it is the same up to 1440p (the frames behind the playhead are kept ready), a 4k file goes back as fast as the browser can seek
- start from an empty timeline or a template (3d title, 3d shapes, lit room, name tag, countdown, lyrics, numbers, snow title, carousel, rack focus, curved wall, block title, tunnel, letter ring, orbit, pivots, letters fly in, timer)
- everything stays on your machine: projects and media live in indexeddb. save a project as one `.ncut` file (project + media + the font files you imported for it) to back it up or move it to another computer. an export tells you when a font of the project is missing on this machine
- monochrome, high contrast, flat ui in kode mono. follows your system: black at night, white by day. optional color tint in settings. no glow.
- wallpaper: pick a picture in the settings and it shows behind the whole ui (projects screen and editor). fill / fit / stretch / center / tile, a dim and a blur slider, and how solid the panels are. the picture stays on your machine, and it never ends up in an export
- a small fps counter in the corner so you can see how the editor is performing
- tools say what they are: rest the pointer on a button or tab, or tab to it, and a tip shows its name, what it does, its keys and, when it would do nothing now, why. the keyboard shortcuts (?) can be searched and list every mouse move. search actions (ctrl+k) finds every action, also the ones in a right click menu, by its old name too. the clip menu opens from the properties header and with shift+f10 or the menu key, the track icon opens the track menu, and project shows sky, fog and light without losing the selection
- mcp server (desktop app only, off by default): lets claude code and other mcp clients work in the open window, add and edit clips, look at a frame and export. turn it on in the settings (the gear or ctrl+, in the editor and on the projects screen) and copy the connect command from there. while a client works you see its cursor in the window, with its name and what it is doing: it flies to the clip, the track, the time or the spot on the canvas the client touches. several agents at once (an agent and its subagents) each get their own cursor in their own color, up to 8. the client can also point at something and leave a note. "show cursors" in the settings switches them off. see [docs/MCP.md](MCP.md)
- auto update (desktop app only, on by default): the exe asks github now and then whether a newer release is out, downloads it in the background, checks it against the checksum github gives for it, and installs it when you close ncut. "restart to update" in the settings does it at once. the installed app runs its setup silently, the portable exe is replaced in place and keeps its name. one anonymous question to github, nothing about you or your projects is sent. the switch "update automatically" turns it off, "check now" still works then. see [docs/UPDATE.md](UPDATE.md)
- words ncut uses. clip: a thing on the timeline. media: a file you imported (never "asset" in ui text; "file" only for files on your computer). layers: the tab that lists the clips on screen. split: one clip becomes several (at the playhead, at the markers, into letters, words or lines, into captions). trim: an edge of a clip moves (trim start, trim end). cut: the clipboard (copy, then delete). a cut in the transition texts is where one clip meets the next. crop: hide the edges of the picture. delete: clips and markers go away. remove: something comes off or out (a track, an effect, a mask, a transition, the sky, media). clear: all of a kind at once (keyframes, beat markers, the tempo). attach / detach: a clip moves, rotates and scales with another clip. group / ungroup: clips select, move, copy and delete together. never "link", "parent" or "follow" for either. scale: the size of a clip. scale x / y / z: stretch one axis. zoom: only the view (timeline, preview), never a clip. tilt (around x), turn (around y), rotation (around z, the only one a flat clip has). "rotate" is the general verb, "turn" is only the 3d label. roll only for the camera. pan: the camera turning left or right, and for a sound (a clip or a track) where it sits between the left and the right speaker. strength: how strong an effect is. amount: only the size of a wiggle. shutter: motion blur. look at: which way a 3d clip or the camera points. never "face" for it. fit / fill: always with what it fits (fit to canvas, fit to panel, fit to the markers, fit to clip, zoom to fit). loop: plays again. in point / out point: where the timeline range starts and ends (i marks the first frame inside, o the last); the source view's media marks use the same words. in to out: the part between them, never "work area" or "region". mark: set an in or out point ("mark the beats" stays the markers). lift: take out what is between in and out, the gap stays (in the grade, lift is the dark wheel). extract: take it out and close up. source view: the preview's view of one media file before it is on the timeline (shift+v, a click on a card), never "monitor" or "viewer". marked part: what a media's in and out keep. match frame: f, the clip's media at the same frame, and back. skim: the mouse over a video card shows its frames. side by side: the source view next to the output. picture only / sound only: one half of a video with sound. active view: the one with the outline, it takes the keys. target: the track an edit from the source view puts its picture or sound on (the crosshair in a track header, auto by default; the bar shows where a clip will land). insert: the marked part goes in, the clips after it move later. overwrite: it covers what is there, nothing moves. replace: a clip shows other media, frame matched, same length. fit to the range: the marked part fills in to out at another speed, never "fit to fill". append: after the last clip of the target tracks. repeat: makes copies. a keyframe tool that plays them twice is "play twice". keyframe: a stored value at a time (the diamond). key: the verb. color key, chroma key and green screen keep their meaning. mute: no sound (clip, track, preview). never "silence". output: what gets exported. scene: the 3d world from a free camera. camera view: looking through the camera clip. output inset: the small output picture in the scene view. effect clip: what other apps call an adjustment layer. settings: the one dialog (app and project). bend: a clip rolls round a tube. never warp, curve, wrap, flex or deform for it. curve is only the text curve. pivot: the point a clip rotates and scales around. never anchor, origin, joint, bone or rig. ripple trim: an edge moves and what follows moves with it, no gap. edit / edit point: where one clip meets the next on a track (never "cut" for it, "cut" stays the clipboard). move the edit: the edit moves, one clip gets longer and the other shorter (never roll or rolling edit; roll stays the camera's). slip: same place and length, another part of the media. slide: the clip moves and its neighbours trim to stay touching. stretch: in the timeline the speed changes so the same media fills a new length (scale x / y / z stays the picture). trim mode: the timeline switch for these. track height: how tall a track is on the timeline (drag the bottom edge of its header; taller tracks / shorter tracks change all; normal height goes back). a view setting, never an undo step. move track up / down: its place in the list; a video track higher up draws on top. sync lock: a track's switch, on by default: ripple, insert and extract on other tracks move it too, so it stays in sync. off: it stays where it is (an edit on the track itself still moves it). out of sync: a picture and its sound no longer play the same moment. never "sync" alone for the switch, never "link". selected edge: an edge picked in trim mode that , and . trim. nudge: a small step by key: alt+left / alt+right move the selected clips (or the selected edge) a frame, shift ten; in the preview the arrows move a clip a pixel. typed time: 1:30:00, 90f or 12.5s goes there, + / - moves by it; a whole number is frames, a point means seconds. timecode / frames / seconds: how times show in the transport and on the ruler. edit, for up / down: any clip start or end. sound clip: an audio clip that plays the sound of a video. picture: the video clip of a pair. separate sound / join sound back: a video's sound becomes a clip of its own on an audio track, and goes back. never "link", "unlink", "detach", "split" or "extract" for it. in sync: picture and sound play the same moment of the file. move into sync / slip into sync: line them up by moving the clip or by sliding its media. j cut / l cut: the sound starts before / ends after the picture. mixer: the tab with one strip per track that has sound, plus the output strip. fader: the level of a track or of the output, in db (0 db = as recorded, -inf = off). solo: hear only some tracks, for listening only (never saved, never in the export). meter: how loud it is while playing. over: past 0 db, the loudest a file holds. output strip: the level of the whole mix, what gets exported, never master volume. db is written lowercase. scopes: the measuring pictures next to the preview (waveform, parade, vectorscope, histogram). waveform: in the scopes, how bright each column of the picture is; on a clip, the shape of its sound. parade: the waveform of r, g and b side by side. luma: how bright a pixel is, 0 to 100. live and exact (in the scopes only): live reads the preview picture, exact reads the frame drawn at the full project size, like the export. probe: hold alt over the picture to read one pixel. never "ire" in ui text. grade: a clip's grade, its section in the look tab, and the grade effect. lift, gamma, gain, offset: the four wheels, for the dark parts, the middle, the bright parts and every tone. level: red, green and blue of a wheel together. curves: only inside the grade, by their tab names (luma, red, green, blue, hue vs sat, hue vs hue, luma vs sat). "curve" alone stays the text curve, "graph" the keyframe graph, never "curve editor". point: a point on the curves. grade on: the switch that shows a clip with or without its grade (search also finds bypass). copy grade, paste grade, reset grade: the whole grade at once. numbers: the red, green and blue fields of each wheel. crossfade: the sound of one clip fades out while the sound of the next fades in, across the edit between them (the picture blend stays the transition "cross fade"). media past the edit: the media before a clip's first frame or after its last, what a crossfade plays. fade shape: how a sound fade or a crossfade runs (the "shape" row in the fades), the picture always fades straight. equal power: keeps the loudness of two different sounds, the crossfade default. linear: straight, for two parts of one take, the default of a clip fade. smooth: slow at both ends. place: centered on, starts at or ends at the edit. picked crossfade: the one a click picked: delete removes it, , and . change it a frame each side. keyframe panel: the rows under the tracks, one per animated value of the selected clips. selected keyframes are the filled diamonds there. graph: the second view of the keyframe panel, one line per animated value. speed: how fast a value changes, always with /s. influence: how far a handle reaches, in %. custom: the ease of a part shaped with handles. ease into / ease out of: the slow part is at the selected keyframe. lut: a look up table from a .cube file, the ui word (never "input lut", "3d lut" only in the size line). mix: how much of the lut. lut first / lut last: its place in the grade. made for 0 to 255 / made for 16 to 235: never "range" or "level" for it. key frame: a whole picture in the file, where a player can start (not a keyframe of an animation). key frame interval: how often the file holds one. codec: how the file packs the picture (h.264, h.265, av1, vp9) or the sound (aac, opus). bitrate: how much of the file each second gets, mbps for the picture, kbps for the sound. sample rate: sound samples per second, 44.1 or 48 khz. fit to the size / fill the size: a size of another shape shows the whole picture with bars, or covers the file and cuts the edges. see-through background: no background where nothing is drawn, webm and png only (search also finds transparent and alpha). loudness: how loud the mix sounds over time, in lufs (lu for a difference or a range, dbtp for a true peak). loudness strip: the live mixer strip beside the output strip: m (momentary, 0.4 s), s (short term, 3 s), i (integrated, since the reset), lra (loudness range), tp (true peak); close, not exact. measure mix: renders the sound and gives the numbers of record. loudness target: the loudness the export gains the mix to (off by default). peak limit: the true peak the export keeps under (-1 dbtp), held by a limiter. never "normalize" or "master" for either. matte: a clip that decides where another clip shows, by its outline (alpha) or its brightness (luma). the matte clip is not drawn on its own. never "track matte" (it is a clip, not a track) and never "link".
