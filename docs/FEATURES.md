# everything ncut does

the long list. the short one is in the [readme](../README.md).

- multi-track timeline: video, image, text, shape and audio clips. move, trim (drag an edge with several clips selected and they all trim together; `[` and `]` or the toolbar trim the start or the end to the playhead), drop a clip, text, shape, effect or camera from the media panel inbetween two clips (the clips after it move right to make room, in one undo step), drop a clip on the border between two tracks and a new track opens there, split, ripple delete, duplicate, copy/paste, snapping, markers, markers on the beats of a song, groups, freeze frame (the still keeps the mask, the effects and the fades of the clip), undo/redo
- the properties of a clip are split in tabs: basic (name, timing, the main settings, transform, attach, speed, fades), look (style, color, mask, effects, 3d, bend), animate (keyframes, motion, path, repeat, transition) and sound (volume, tempo). "all" is the one long list. the tab you pick stays while you switch clips, left / right arrows move between tabs
- every number in the properties is a slider: drag it sideways (shift is finer, ctrl lands on round steps), click to type (1920/2 works, a comma works), double click to put it back to its rest value. the fill shows where the value sits in its usual range, a thick end means it is past it, dragging on goes further. a drag is one undo step and esc takes it back. number fields show the real value instead of cutting it short (a 70 frame clip at 60 fps shows 1.1667 s), and leaving a field changes nothing if the text is the same as what was shown. with several clips selected, position, scale, rotation, opacity, depth, tilt, turn, volume, fades and effect strength work for all of them: a drag moves each one by the same amount (scale by the same factor), a typed number sets them all
- a plain color card is always the first thing in the media tab, in every project, with nothing to import: a full frame picture of one color (pick the color on the card, change it later in the properties). a background, a flash, or a plane for 3d
- real-time preview on a canvas, with on-canvas move / scale / rotate, zoom and pan, guides, and a quality switch (full / half / quarter) for heavy timelines. preview speed from 0.25x to 2x and j / k / l shuttle in both directions
- keyframes on every number of a clip, each with its own diamond, previous / next key and easing: position, scale, rotation, opacity, volume, effect settings, camera, and also corner radius, crop edges, color correction, color key, mask (position, size, turn, soft edge, corners), 3d thickness, how lit (3d), a text's size, spacing, line gap, outline, shadow, depth, gradient angle, curve, wrap and box, and a shape's size, corners, inner radius, gradient angle and outline width. every number has a key list of its own, a key on one number never touches another. a number inside something that has to be switched on first (mask, color key, outline, shadow, depth, gradient, lit) can be keyed once that is on. tools for a whole animation: reverse it, fit it to the clip, repeat it, ping pong, copy it to another clip, or add a wiggle (a seeded shake on any value)
- a stretch per axis on top of scale: scale x and scale y make the box wider or taller (the picture or the letters stretch with it, a shape's outline keeps an even width), scale z is the depth of a 3d solid or of extruded text. all three can be keyed. a clip attached to a stretched clip follows its scale only, never its stretch
- bpm: a clip with sound has a tempo. ncut listens and finds it (detect), or type it, or tap along, with half / double time and a "first beat" nudge, and the beats show as ticks on the clip. the keyframes of any clip can then follow that beat: loop the first 1/2, 1, 2, 4, 8 or 16 beats until the clip ends, ping pong, fit the whole animation to a number of beats, or snap every keyframe to the nearest beat, half beat or quarter beat. edit the first round of a loop and press loop again to carry it through. effect clips follow the tempo too (pulse on the beat in their inspector): pick whose tempo (or the sound under the effect), a cycle of 1/4 to 16 beats, and a pulse that hits at full strength on every cycle line and falls off until the next one. effects that repeat on their own (flash, zoom pulse, glitch, static, scanlines, hue wash and more) also have a lock that makes one of their cycles one beat cycle. it is worked out when a frame is drawn, so it stays right when the effect or the music is moved, trimmed or split
- 3d, set up like a small game engine scene: any clip can become a plane, box, ball, tube, cone, pyramid, ring, capsule, prism, hexagon, star or heart in one shared 3d space, mixed freely with flat 2d layers. camera clips move through it (with ready-made moves: push in, orbit, dolly zoom, handheld), a scene view lets you orbit around the set with move / rotate / scale tools, driven like blender by default (middle drag orbits, g / r / s move, rotate and scale without holding a button, x / y / z pick the axis, a typed number sets the amount, shift + d copies, a left drag draws a selection box, the numpad picks the view) or like unity with a switch in the settings (left drag orbits, w e r pick the tool, alt + drag leaves a copy); ctrl snaps to a grid in both while a small inset shows what the camera sees, distance fog fades far things out, lit clips take their light from the scene (the background, the filled 2d shapes under the lowest 3d clip and the fog; pictures, video and text are not read) or from a light of your own (sky, horizon, ground and a sun). light only darkens or tints: a white surrounding leaves a clip as it is, on the default black background a lit clip gets darker, and its how lit slider softens that, and the camera has depth of field: pick what is sharp, keyframe it to pull the focus, or drag the focus plane right in the scene view
- skyboxes: import a cube map (one cross or strip picture, or six pictures named like px, posx, right or rt) under project > sky, or press "use as sky" when an import looks like one. it is drawn behind every layer, far away, turns with the camera and gives lit 3d clips its light (a dome and a sun measured from it). turn, brightness and blur can be set, the six sides can be swapped and turned by hand.
- bend a flat picture clip (a 2d clip or a plane) like a cinema screen or a rolled page: an angle (keyable) along its width or height, and where it stays flat. bend several clips as one: a row of pictures, a wall of cards or a text split into letters roll round together into one surface, up to a closed ring, and the bend stays editable and keyable for all of them at once. 2d clips bend too, seen straight on
- attach clips to each other (or to the camera, or to an invisible "empty") so they move, rotate and scale together, see the whole set as a tree in the layers tab, and make any clip look at the camera or another clip. give any clip a pivot, the point it rotates and scales around (a shoulder, a hinge, the middle of a clock): pick one of nine spots, drag it on the preview, or put it where the clip meets the clip it is attached to. with attach this builds a rig: an arm that rotates at the shoulder, the elbow and the wrist. setting a pivot keeps the clip in place at the playhead (a clip that looks at something looks from its pivot, so a big picture can turn a little), and keys, stretch, look at and the ready-made motions all rotate around it. arrange a selection in 3d in one click: ring, row, stack, fan, grid, spiral, sphere, scatter, wave, stairs. put a clip in front of the camera, frame it with the camera, lay it flat or drop it on the floor. repeat a clip: a row of copies, each a step further in position, depth, turn, size, opacity or time (an echo, a staircase, a tunnel). send a clip along a path in one click: circle, figure eight, spiral, swing, hop, zigzag, drift, written as ordinary keyframes, and it can rotate to point the way it moves
- motion blur many ways: a real shutter as an effect clip (centered, trailing, leading), per-clip motion blur that follows keyframes and motions in three kinds (smooth, trail, ghost) on 2d and 3d clips, echo / frame blend, and directional / zoom / spin / tilt shift / lens / soft focus / rgb trail blur
- 94 effects (color, blur, distortion, stylize, motion, looks like sketch, neon edges, hologram, oil paint, glass and led screen, and overlays like snow, rain, confetti, bokeh, light rays, lens flare) as effect clips over everything below (an adjustment layer), or put on one clip only (a blur on a text really goes soft at the edges, a shake or a glitch moves its outline too), a chroma key effect that makes one color see-through (on a track of its own it keys the video or picture right under it), with favorites, and 87 transitions (fades, slides, pushes, wipes, 3d turns, glitches) you drop on a cut, or put on every cut of a track at once (one kind or shuffled)
- per-clip color (brightness, contrast, saturation, warmth, hue), green screen with spill cleanup, masks with a soft edge (box, oval, half, strip, star, heart), blend modes, crop, rounded corners. split screen in one click: side by side, stacked, grid, one big, picture in picture
- text: 29 bundled fonts plus system fonts and your own font files, outline, second outline, hollow letters, shadow, gradient, block depth (real thickness when the text stands in 3d), underline and strike, a box per line, curved and wrapped text, per-letter animations in, out and looping, karaoke highlighting, split a text into letters, words or lines (every piece becomes its own clip, right where it stood, ready to be arranged in 3d), and running numbers that count up or down, as plain numbers or as a timer (m:ss) with steady digits
- captions: import .srt / .vtt, split a long text into captions, export .srt
- an outline on a 3d solid goes around the solid and along its edges
- shapes with outlines that draw themselves on, and ready-made clip motions in, out and looping (some just for 3d: turntable, tumble, fly in)
- fades, per-clip volume, speed and opacity. swap the media of a clip and keep everything else
- cascade a selection: start the clips one after the other (left to right, middle outwards, random ...) and let them fly in or out (scatter, rise, fall, zoom, spin, from the depth). made for a text split into letters
- cut to the music: mark the beats of a song, then lay the selected clips in a row, fit them to the markers (every beat, every 2nd, every 4th) or split a clip at every marker
- export to mp4 or webm with webcodecs at up to 240 fps, gif, or audio only (wav / ogg), whole timeline or a range, with one click presets. no upload, no watermark. what you see in the preview is what gets exported
- export a picture: png or jpg of the first frame (or the frame at a marker or at any time), in the project's full size or smaller
- fast export: longer videos are rendered in parts by several workers at once and joined into one file, 1440p and 4k go to the graphics card's encoder at any frame rate, gifs are built on up to 8 workers, big files are written to disk while they are made instead of sitting in memory
- smooth scrubbing: dragging the playhead forward through a video shows about 50 to 60 pictures a second, also with 4k files (a decoder of its own keeps going while you drag, the resting picture is exact). backwards it is the same up to 1440p (the frames behind the playhead are kept ready), a 4k file goes back as fast as the browser can seek
- start from an empty timeline or a template (3d title, 3d shapes, lit room, name tag, countdown, lyrics, numbers, snow title, carousel, rack focus, curved wall, block title, tunnel, letter ring, orbit, pivots, letters fly in, timer)
- everything stays on your machine: projects and media live in indexeddb. save a project as one `.ncut` file (project + media + the font files you imported for it) to back it up or move it to another computer. an export tells you when a font of the project is missing on this machine
- monochrome, high contrast, flat ui in kode mono. follows your system: black at night, white by day. optional color tint in settings. no glow.
- wallpaper: pick a picture in the settings and it shows behind the whole ui (projects screen and editor). fill / fit / stretch / center / tile, a dim and a blur slider, and how solid the panels are. the picture stays on your machine, and it never ends up in an export
- a small fps counter in the corner so you can see how the editor is performing
- tools say what they are: rest the pointer on a button or tab, or tab to it, and a tip shows its name, what it does, its keys and, when it would do nothing now, why. the keyboard shortcuts (?) can be searched and list every mouse move. search actions (ctrl+k) finds every action, also the ones in a right click menu, by its old name too. the clip menu opens from the properties header and with shift+f10 or the menu key, the track icon opens the track menu, and project shows sky, fog and light without losing the selection
- mcp server (desktop app only, off by default): lets claude code and other mcp clients work in the open window, add and edit clips, look at a frame and export. turn it on in the settings (the gear or ctrl+, in the editor and on the projects screen) and copy the connect command from there. while a client works you see its cursor in the window, with its name and what it is doing: it flies to the clip, the track, the time or the spot on the canvas the client touches. several agents at once (an agent and its subagents) each get their own cursor in their own color, up to 8. the client can also point at something and leave a note. "show cursors" in the settings switches them off. see [docs/MCP.md](MCP.md)
- auto update (desktop app only, on by default): the exe asks github now and then whether a newer release is out, downloads it in the background, checks it against the checksum github gives for it, and installs it when you close ncut. "restart to update" in the settings does it at once. the installed app runs its setup silently, the portable exe is replaced in place and keeps its name. one anonymous question to github, nothing about you or your projects is sent. the switch "update automatically" turns it off, "check now" still works then. see [docs/UPDATE.md](UPDATE.md)
- words ncut uses. clip: a thing on the timeline. media: a file you imported (never "asset" in ui text; "file" only for files on your computer). layers: the tab that lists the clips on screen. split: one clip becomes several (at the playhead, at the markers, into letters, words or lines, into captions). trim: an edge of a clip moves (trim start, trim end). cut: the clipboard (copy, then delete). a cut in the transition texts is where one clip meets the next. crop: hide the edges of the picture. delete: clips and markers go away. remove: something comes off or out (a track, an effect, a mask, a transition, the sky, media). clear: all of a kind at once (keyframes, beat markers, the tempo). attach / detach: a clip moves, rotates and scales with another clip. group / ungroup: clips select, move, copy and delete together. never "link", "parent" or "follow" for either. scale: the size of a clip. scale x / y / z: stretch one axis. zoom: only the view (timeline, preview), never a clip. tilt (around x), turn (around y), rotation (around z, the only one a flat clip has). "rotate" is the general verb, "turn" is only the 3d label. roll and pan only for the camera. strength: how strong an effect is. amount: only the size of a wiggle. shutter: motion blur. look at: which way a 3d clip or the camera points. never "face" for it. fit / fill: always with what it fits (fit to canvas, fit to panel, fit to the markers, fit to clip, zoom to fit). loop: plays again. repeat: makes copies. a keyframe tool that plays them twice is "play twice". keyframe: a stored value at a time (the diamond). key: the verb. color key, chroma key and green screen keep their meaning. mute: no sound (clip, track, preview). never "silence". output: what gets exported. scene: the 3d world from a free camera. camera view: looking through the camera clip. output inset: the small output picture in the scene view. effect clip: what other apps call an adjustment layer. settings: the one dialog (app and project). bend: a clip rolls round a tube. never warp, curve, wrap, flex or deform for it. curve is only the text curve. pivot: the point a clip rotates and scales around. never anchor, origin, joint, bone or rig
